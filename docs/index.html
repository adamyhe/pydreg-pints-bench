pydreg vs PINTS Benchmark
Nascent transcription · TRE calling benchmark · 26 Nov 2026

pydreg 1.0.0 vs PINTS 1.3.0

On 3′ run-on data (PRO-, GRO-, ChRO-seq), pydreg finds more transcribed regulatory elements at strict false-positive rates, and the lead is largest at enhancer-like sites. PINTS is better on TSS assays (PRO-cap, GRO-cap). It also has a modest edge at telling transcribed from untranscribed DHSs at enhancer-like sites.

15 libraries · 5 cell types (K562, GM12878, HCT116, A549, Jurkat) · gaps are pydreg − PINTS with 95 % paired 5 Mb block-bootstrap intervals (1,000 replicates)

Findings

  1. pydreg

    Regulatory elements vs non-regulatory background, 3′ run-on

    Recall at 5 % FPR is +0.08 to +0.25 higher across essentially every run-on library, with intervals excluding zero. The lead grows when negatives are GC-matched, and grows again for non-CpG-island positives (typically +0.15 to +0.40). It holds even when PINTS's own PRO-cap calls are the positives (K562 G7: +0.23 raw, +0.40 non-CpG-island). The weakest case is Jurkat ChRO-seq batch 2, about zero after GC matching.

  2. Close · PINTS on enhancers

    Transcribed vs TSS-silent DHSs

    Among accessible sites, the gaps are within ±0.06 on recall and ±0.03 on AUPRC in K562, GM12878 and HCT116. On non-CpG-island positives PINTS is modestly ahead (K562 G7 −0.06 to −0.12, HCT116 −0.07). pydreg leads on the deep K562 G1 library; GM12878 is a tie. A549 and Jurkat silent sets come from shallow TSS assays and are not reliable.

  3. PINTS

    TSS assays

    PINTS leads on PRO-cap (K562, HCT116, A549) and GRO-cap. HCT116 PRO-cap, H3K27ac/DHS vs quiet: −0.09 raw, −0.13 to −0.33 under GC matching and the CpG split. The exception is the shallow Jurkat CAGE library, where pydreg leads.

  4. Mixed

    PINTS-paper labels, without the read filter

    pydreg leads on K562 run-on (+0.05 to +0.09; +0.12 to +0.19 on non-CpG-island positives). PINTS leads on GRO-cap. GM12878 HiDRA is tied at 5 % FPR, with PINTS ahead at 1 % (point estimate). Most of the dREG deficit reported in the PINTS paper comes from protocol: dREG scored on its default calls only, plus an asymmetric overlap rule.

Run time

PINTS is faster overall: about 2.9 h against 4.6 h for all 15 libraries. On the deepest run-on libraries the two are about even. On TSS assays PINTS is 4–9× faster. pydreg uses far less memory: PINTS grows with depth, up to 58 GB.

How it was scored

  • Element-level, symmetric. Each labelled element takes the max score of any call touching it: pydreg's SVR score over all candidates, and PINTS's −log10 p over all rebuilt divergent pairs.
  • Headline metrics: TPR at 5 % FPR and pAUROC (FPR ≤ 0.1). These sit where genome-wide calling operates and don't depend on the class ratio. TPR at 1 % FPR is a point estimate only, with no interval yet.
  • AUPRC is reported against its baseline. Negative counts are capped or filtered by construction, so compare tools within a set, not across sets. AUROC is supplementary.
  • GC-matched: in every replicate, negatives are reweighted so their GC histogram (20 bins) matches the positives'. GC alone separates positives from negatives with AUROC 0.73–0.91, and matching cost PINTS more than pydreg (median −0.018 vs −0.002 AUROC).
  • Non-CpG-island: positives with GC ≤ 0.5 or CpG o/e ≤ 0.6, scored against GC-matched negatives. These are the enhancer-like elements.

Caveats

  • One library per cell and assay, except Jurkat ChRO-seq. The two ChRO-seq batches differ about as much as PRO-seq differs from ChRO-seq.
  • dREG-paper positives come from dREG's authors; PINTS-on-TSS positives are PINTS calls. H3K27ac/DHS labels are tool-independent but histone-defined.
  • K562 G1 and G2 were in dREG's training data, which is optimistic for pydreg. G7, GM12878, HCT116, A549 and Jurkat were not.
  • Earlier AUROC-based conclusions are superseded. "AUROC tied" readings mostly hid pydreg's lead at strict thresholds.
